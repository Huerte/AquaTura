PROBLEM STATEMENT:
According to the World Health Organization (WHO, 2022), around 600 millionpeople worldwide fall ill each year after eating contaminated food, and approximately420,000 die as a result of food-borne diseases. In the Philippines, the Bureau of
Fisheries and Aquatic Resources (BFAR, 2023) reports that the country producesover
4.3 million metric tons of fish annually, making it one of the top fish-producing nationsinSoutheast Asia. Despite this, seafood-borne illnesses remain a persistent issue caused byinadequate monitoring systems, lack of access to accurate information, and improper
post-harvest handling. Many fisher folk lack the tools to identify fish diseases, whileconsumers and tourists often remain unaware of the safety and origin of their seafood. This disconnect not only threatens public health and marine biodiversity but alsolimitsthe economic potential of sustainable fisheries and coastal tourism. SDG FOCUS:
AquaTura PH aligns with the following United Nations Sustainable Development Goals(SDGs):
SDG 2 – Zero Hunger: Promotes food safety and sustainable seafood supplychains
SDG 12 – Responsible Consumption and Production: Encourages traceability,
transparency, and eco-friendly fishing practices. SDG 14 – Life Below Water: Protects marine ecosystems and supports
responsible fisheries management. MARKET SEGMENT:
AquaTura PH targets an integrated market ecosystem consisting of:
 Fisherfolk and aquaculture producers who need accessible fish health monitoringtools.  Seafood consumers seeking transparency and safety assurance;

 Local government units (LGUs) and DA-BFAR partners for fisheries data andbiodiversity tracking:
 Restaurants and tourism stakeholders aiming to promote locally sourced, traceableseafood. This multi-sectoral approach bridges technology, livelihood, and sustainability—building a transparent digital network that benefits both communities and the
environment. PROPOSED SOLUTION:
AquaTura PH is a green-tech mobile application powered by AI-driven imagerecognition that allows users to scan and identify fish species, detect potential diseases, and trace the fish’s origin. Using cloud-based data integration and verified partnershipswith government and research institutions, the platform ensures that marine dataisreliable and accessible to all. Each scan contributes to a shared data ecosystem, supporting:
 Fisherfolk in disease detection and management,  Agencies in tracking fish biodiversity and stock conditions, and:
 Businesses in promoting sustainable and transparent seafood sourcing. By creating a circular value model, AquaTura PH strengthens food safety, boostsfisherfolk livelihoods, and enhances marine tourism. Through collaboration with DA-BFAR, LGUs, and private coastal enterprises, theplatform aims to expand nationwide — transforming local fishing towns into digital
tourism and sustainability hubs.
